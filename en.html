JSPS KAKENHI Grant No. 23K11585 | Social Support Networks of Foreign Muslim Women Raising Children in Japan (FY2023–2027)
Migration · Maternity · Multicultural Support

Rethinking support in Japan through what is “normal” at home.

This research focuses on migrant Muslim women in Japan and examines pregnancy, childbirth and child-rearing in relation to healthcare cultures in their countries of origin.

Starting pointCultural and religious differences should not become reasons for exclusion from necessary support.

ApproachConnecting women, communities and professionals with healthcare practices in countries of origin.

GoalSystems in which women are respected without having to explain themselves from the beginning every time.

Purpose of This Website

Sharing knowledge for research and practice

This website shares research findings and examples of practice concerning the difficulties experienced by Muslim women living in Japan during pregnancy, childbirth, and child-rearing, as well as the support they need.

It aims to serve as a platform for researchers, practitioners, and community members interested in this and related fields to deepen their knowledge, learn about initiatives in Japan and other countries, and gain insights for future research, support practices, and community-based activities.

The Research Question

From lived experience to systems of support

What difficulties do women encounter while experiencing pregnancy, childbirth and child-rearing across cultures, whom do they connect with, and what support do they need?

The study considers relationships with family, ethnic communities, health and welfare systems, the Japanese language, and cultural and religious backgrounds. It explores culturally safe conditions that enable women’s voices to be heard and support to be accessed with confidence.

Research Purpose

Building a shared foundation for culturally responsive support

This research examines the daily difficulties, support needs, and social support networks of foreign Muslim women living in Japan during pregnancy, childbirth, and child-rearing.

It also investigates maternal and child healthcare cultures, customs, family involvement, and religious norms in countries and regions of origin. The study aims to generate knowledge that can help health, welfare, and maternal and child health professionals accumulate and share culturally informed support methods and examples of practice.

Fieldwork · Dhaka

Understanding maternal healthcare culture on site

In March 2025, visits were conducted to two private hospitals, one public maternity hospital and one maternal and child health facility in Dhaka. Interviews showed differences in facilities, congestion, costs, explanations and family involvement.

Doctoral Research

Comparison across three regional groups

Participants were classified into Indonesia, South Asia, and the Middle East/Other regions. Group differences were examined using the Kruskal–Wallis test.

Types of social support

Statistically significant differences among the three groups were found in informational and emotional support. The Indonesian group showed higher average scores than the South Asian group.

Sources of social support

Significant group differences were found in support from non-Muslim Japanese friends, Muslim friends from the same country, and professional consultation services and hospitals. The Indonesian group showed higher average scores than the South Asian group.

The test identifies overall three-group differences; details of individual group comparisons are provided in the dissertation.

Future Research

Extending the research to Indonesia

Both Bangladeshi and Indonesian women participated in earlier interviews and questionnaires. Future fieldwork in Indonesia will examine maternal healthcare culture, family support, religious and cultural contexts, healthcare access, and child-rearing.

Drawing on healthcare cultures in Bangladesh and Indonesia and the experiences of Muslim women living in Japan, the research will examine the conditions needed for support professionals to accumulate and share relevant knowledge and support methods.
